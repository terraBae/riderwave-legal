Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 17, 2026 · Applies to Rider Wave (Android)

Plain-language summary: Rider Wave has no analytics and no advertising, and you can use it without an account. Without an account, your rides, routes and saved places stay on your phone. Some map features can be answered by the Rider Wave Server, which we run ourselves. When you use those, the server sees your IP address and the location or search text needed to answer, and keeps a basic access log, without those contents, for a short time, usually a few days. We never sell personal information. We may sell or license combined, anonymous ride-path data from all riders together, never data about you as an individual (section 8). We give information to law enforcement only when the law requires it (section 14), and police officers can use Rider Wave like any other rider (section 15). Rider Wave accounts, profiles, business profiles and community features are optional and in beta. If you create an account, the information you add, and anything you choose to share such as your location, heading, speed, route or destination, is stored on our server and may be seen by other riders. While these features are in beta, the privacy settings that limit who sees it may not work correctly, so treat anything you add to an account as visible to other riders. Section 13 explains this in full. You must be 16 or older to create an account. By using Rider Wave you accept this policy as part of the Terms of Use.

1. What this app is

Rider Wave is a motorcycle navigation and ride-tracking app under active development. Accounts are optional and in beta (section 13); everything in sections 1 to 12 works without one. Without an account, your rides and settings are created and stored on your phone. For some map features the app can talk to the Rider Wave Server, described in section 4. Versions of Rider Wave published on Google Play or the App Store before November 2026 never contacted that server, and section 4 does not apply to them.

2. Data stored on your phone

The app stores the following in its own private storage on your phone:

  • Profile information you enter (name, rider icon, bike/garage entries)
  • Saved routes and ride history (GPS tracks, distance, time, speed)
  • Hazard reports you create
  • Saved places (home, work, favorites, recent searches and destinations)
  • Map pictures and weather data that the map keeps on your phone so it loads faster
  • Diagnostic log (off by default; Settings → Developer): if you turn it on, the app keeps a log of what it is doing, including your precise position and the time of each point, so problems can be investigated. It is held on your phone and leaves it only if you export it and choose where to send it
  • App settings

Depending on your device settings, Android's backup feature may include this data in your Google backup. You can delete this data at any time from Settings → Your data in the app, or by deleting the app.

3. Location data

With your permission, Rider Wave uses your phone's location to show your position on the map, give turn-by-turn directions, and record your ride while the app is open. It asks for location only while the app is in use. While you are navigating a route, the app keeps using your location in the background, with a notification showing your next turn, so directions keep working with the screen locked or another app in front; this stops when the route ends or you close the app. Otherwise it does not use your location in the background or while the screen is locked. Using location in the background sends nothing more than navigating with the app open. Your ride track and position history are stored only on your phone. Location is sent off the phone only in the ways described in sections 4, 5 and 6, to answer a map request you make, and, if you have an account, when you choose to share it as described in section 13.

4. The Rider Wave Server

Rider Wave runs its own self-hosted server, operated by the app's developer on its own hardware in the United States, that can answer some map requests so that fewer of them go to outside companies. These features are listed below. All of them can be turned off in Settings → Developer; most are off by default, and "Search as you type" is on by default.

  • Routes (off by default): the start, stops and destination of a route that lies within Florida and Kentucky, and the direction you are heading when you are moving (so the route starts the right way), are sent so the server can calculate it. If you ask for a way home that avoids highways or tolls, or that uses different roads from the ride, the app sends the same kind of request; for "different roads" it also sends up to 40 points along the roads you just rode, so the server can steer away from them. Other routes use the public routing services in section 6.
  • Address search (off by default): your search text and your position, used to rank nearby results.
  • Search as you type (on by default): the letters you have typed so far, and your position, to suggest places while you type. If you turn it off, searching works as before when you press Enter.
  • Weather radar (off by default): the radar map tiles for the area you are viewing.
  • Traffic rerouting suggestions (off by default): see section 5.

What the server records. Its access log keeps your IP address, the date and time, the type of request, the web address path, the response status, its size, how long it took and a few other technical details of the connection. For radar the path includes the map tile coordinates, so the log can show roughly which area was viewed. The log does not record the contents of requests (route coordinates, search text), your request headers, or query strings. Log entries are kept for a short time, usually a few days, and then deleted. The log is rotated by size, so on a very quiet server an entry could stay longer, but we aim to keep none for more than about a week. We use them only to keep the server secure, to limit abuse (for example, too many requests from one address) and to fix problems.

What the server does not do for these map features. They do not need an account, are not linked to one, do not identify you, and are not used to build profiles of riders. The app contains a shared access key that stops strangers from using the server; it is the same for every copy of the app and does not identify you. Connections use HTTPS.

5. Traffic rerouting suggestions and HERE

If you turn on "Traffic rerouting suggestions" (Settings → Developer), then while you are navigating Rider Wave sends your current position, the direction you are heading when you are moving, your destination, a few points along your route and roughly how far you have left to go to the Rider Wave Server every few minutes. The server asks HERE Technologies, a traffic data provider, for current traffic along that route and sends back only whether there is a slowdown and a possible faster route.

With the same switch on, the map also shows traffic on your route: when you look at a route before starting it, and every few minutes while you navigate, the app asks the Rider Wave Server for traffic in a few map squares of about 30 km that your route passes through (only the squares, not your position or destination). The server asks HERE for slow roads, closures, crashes and roadworks in those squares and passes them back, and the app draws them on the map. The server keeps these answers in memory for about two minutes and does not store them.

Traffic along the whole route. In some versions, instead of asking for map squares, the app sends the Rider Wave Server a simplified line of your route (up to 300 points, without times) every few minutes while you navigate and when you look at a route before starting it. The server asks HERE for traffic speeds and incidents along that whole line and sends back which stretches are slow and which incidents lie on or near the route. HERE receives the route line (and a few points along it to match the road) from our server, not your name or any identifier. The server keeps the answer in memory for about two minutes and does not store your route.

HERE receives those route coordinates, and the direction you are heading (so that facing the wrong way at the start of a route is not mistaken for a delay), from our server. When your phone does not send a direction, for example because you are stopped, the server works one out from the route's own points. It never receives your IP address, and the app has no account or device identifier to send it. The server keeps the answer in memory for about two minutes, so that nearby riders can share it, and does not store it. Rider Wave never changes your route unless you tap "Re-route". HERE's own privacy policy applies to what HERE receives.

6. Other services, sharing and links

When a feature is not using the Rider Wave Server, your phone sends the request directly to an independent service. Rider Wave does not operate these services, and each has its own privacy policy. They see your IP address, as any website does, and the information the request needs: for example the coordinates of a route and the direction you are heading, the area of the map you are viewing, or the place you searched for.

  • Nominatim / OpenStreetMap: address search and geocoding
  • Valhalla and OSRM: route calculation
  • Overpass API (OpenStreetMap): places nearby, such as gas stations
  • Open-Meteo: weather
  • U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): weather alerts
  • RainViewer: radar imagery
  • Map tile providers, including OpenFreeMap, OpenStreetMap-based sources and Esri: the map imagery itself
  • CARTO: backup map imagery, used if the main map style cannot be loaded
  • Google Play (Android only): checks for app updates, using Google's own update service

Rider Wave uses no account or key of its own with these services.

Map pre-loading. To make the map feel instant, the app downloads map pictures ahead of time for the area around your current location: about 20 square miles for every map style when you open the map style picker (including Esri satellite imagery, even if you do not pick it), a wider area if you stay on a style, and the area around you for the weather view. Those requests go to the map providers listed above, and they show roughly where you are.

Sharing and links. When you tap Share (for example on a ride, or "Share as GPX" on a saved route, which makes a route file), your phone's share sheet sends the text, link or file you chose to the app or person you choose; some of it may contain a place's coordinates, for example an OpenStreetMap link. Links such as "Official page", the website or this policy open in your browser or another app, which then sees what any website sees. Once you share something or follow a link, that app's or site's own privacy policy applies.

7. Music control bar

If you grant Notification Access, Rider Wave can display and control whatever music app you are currently playing (song title, artist, album art, play/pause/skip). This works by reading the Android system's local media session on your device. It never reads notification content, never requires logging into any music service, and this data never leaves your device.

8. No analytics, no advertising; how we may use combined ride data

Rider Wave includes no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking software. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for advertising. We do not share what we receive except as described in sections 4 to 6 and 13, as this section describes, or if the law requires it (section 14).

Combined ride-path data. We may sell or license data about how riders use roads, drawn from the activity of the Rider Wave user base as a whole, to other companies, for example companies that want to understand where and how motorcyclists ride, including for marketing. What may be included is limited to ride-path data: the routes and roads ridden, shown together for many riders at once. It is never data about one rider. It is not linked to your name, handle, profile, account, phone, internet address, bikes, posts, messages, follows, searches or saved places, and we will not sell any of those. We will not sell an individual rider's track, and we will not tell a buyer who rode a path. We do not sell your personal information. The ride-path data we may sell is combined so that it describes riders as a group and cannot be used to pick out any one person. The only ride paths we can use are ones that reach our server, for example a ride you choose to upload or share; rides that stay on your phone are never part of it. Routes you publish to the route library are public content, shown to riders as your route, and we do not include them in the combined ride-path data we sell or license.

9. How long we keep data, and your choices

  • On your phone: until you delete it in Settings → Your data or delete the app.
  • On the Rider Wave Server: access log entries are kept for a short time, usually a few days (we aim for no more than about a week), and then deleted. Without an account we store nothing else about you. If you have an account, section 13 lists what the server keeps and for how long.
  • You can turn off every server feature in Settings → Developer, deny or withdraw location permission in your system settings, or stop using the app.
  • If you have an account, you can delete it in the app at any time (section 13).
  • Map features are not linked to an account, so we generally cannot tell which log entries belong to you. If you would like to ask what we hold, or ask us to delete something, email us (section 12) and we will help where we can. If you live where privacy laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act apply: we do not sell or share personal information. The combined ride-path data described in section 8 is not about any one person.

10. Security

We take reasonable steps to protect the server, including HTTPS, request limits and keeping the access log short-lived. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise it will never be compromised.

11. Children's privacy

Rider Wave is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. You must be at least 16 years old to create a Rider Wave account. During testing we do not check your age: by creating an account you confirm you are 16 or older. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, or you believe a child has created one, contact us (section 12) and we will delete it.

Questions about this policy, or requests about data, can be sent to david@terrabae.com.

13. Rider Wave accounts, profiles and community features (beta)

Accounts are optional. Rider Wave works without one, and nothing in this section applies to you unless you create an account. You must be at least 16 years old to create an account. During testing Rider Wave does not check your age; by creating an account you confirm that you are 16 or older, and we delete any account we learn belongs to someone younger. Accounts are being tested and may not be available to you yet. When you create one, the app shows you the Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy and asks you to accept both before the account is made. Creating an account means you have read and accepted them, including the notice below about privacy settings.

What the Rider Wave Server keeps when you have an account. This is the list as the server works today; it keeps nothing about you beyond it, apart from the short-lived access log described in section 4:

  • Your profile: handle (your public username), display name, bio, avatar colour, up to five links, home region (city and state only), the date you started riding, your unit preference, and your settings: who may see each part of your profile (public, followers or private; new settings start private), whether you appear in rider search (off until you turn it on) and whether people need your approval to follow you (on until you change it).
  • Your garage: for each bike a nickname, make, model, year, colour, type and a short description. Rider Wave does not store VINs today; if it ever does, they will be encrypted and shown only to you, and this policy will say so first.
  • Business pages you create or join: name, handle, category, tagline, description, website; locations with address, map position, phone number, opening hours and amenities; who is on the team and their roles; invitations; the note you write when you ask for a business to be verified, or, if you verify by website, the address you give (the server then fetches one small file from that website, or looks up one DNS record, and keeps the address and a one-way fingerprint of the code until it expires or the business is deleted); and claims you make on a place on the map. Business pages are public by design, and a verified business can appear in search and in the nearby list.
  • Who you follow, who follows you and pending follow requests. For a follow made as a business, the server also keeps which person acted; it is never shown to anyone.
  • Your posts (updates, shared rides, and for businesses offers and events) with who may see each one, your comments, your likes and your replies to events. A shared ride is only the name you give it, a simplified line of at most 400 points with the start and end trimmed by the app, the distance, the time it took and the date; never your exact track or the time of each point. Posts and comments you make as a business also record which person wrote them, which is never shown to anyone except the business's owners and managers.
  • The route library: routes you publish (name, description, kinds of ride, difficulty and surface you pick, optional times to avoid or seasons when it is closed, and the route's line of up to 3,000 points with the first and last 500 metres (about a third of a mile) left off by the app, so a home or garage at either end is not shown), with your handle as the author and the date; the routes you save from the library; your star ratings; a single yes that you completed a route (never your track, positions or times); and the comments you write on routes. Published routes, ratings counts and comments are public to anyone using the app; a published route waits for review before it appears, and reviewers can remove it. Importing a GPX file reads it on your phone only.
  • People you block, and reports you make about a rider, a business, a post, a comment or a route: what it is about, the reason you pick and the note you may add. Only the reviewers read reports.
  • Your phones and sign-ins: for each phone a name you choose, platform, app version, when it was last used, a public key created on your phone, and its sign-in sessions. The recovery code is never stored; only a protected check value of it, how and when you backed it up, an estimate of how strong it is, and records of recovery requests.
  • The terms version you accepted and when, and a log of account events: created, recovery started, cancelled or completed, data export requested or downloaded, and actions a reviewer took on your account (such as a suspension or a business being verified). After you delete your account that log keeps only a scrambled form of the account's id that cannot be linked back to you.
  • Your requests for a copy of your data: the server notes that you asked, so the download link works once within 24 hours. The copy itself is built when you download it and is not kept.
  • Counts of requests per internet address, held in the server's memory only, to slow down abuse; they are lost when the server restarts.

Used once and never stored or logged: what you type into rider, business and route-library search, the filters you choose there, and the position the app sends to find nearby businesses. For each business, the server keeps only daily totals of how many times its page was viewed and its buttons tapped, without recording who viewed or tapped; its owners and managers can see them.

Not available yet. Photos, hazard reports and sharing your live position are not available today and the server stores none of them. When one arrives, it will store only the kind of information named in its description here, and this policy will be updated before it goes live. Rider Wave sends no notifications through Google or Apple, and nothing about your account goes to any other company.

We never ask for your email address, phone number, birthday, home address or contacts to create an account.

Who can see it. Your handle, display name and avatar can be seen by anyone using the app, signed in or not. Everything else follows the privacy settings in the app: public, followers (people whose follow request you accepted) or private. A post is either public or for your followers only; a public post can be seen by anyone using the app, signed in or not. A rider you block, and a rider who blocks you, cannot see each other's pages. Reviewers (currently the app's developer, who may use an AI assistant to help read and sort reports) can see reports, including who made them and what they say, business verification notes and the pages they concern. The people who run the Rider Wave Server can technically see anything stored on it, except your recovery code, which it does not have.

Beta notice: privacy settings may not work. Accounts, profiles, business profiles, community features and their privacy settings are in beta testing and have not been fully tested. The settings that control who can see your information are provided for testing only. Because of bugs or unfinished features, information you add to an account or share through it, including your home region, your bikes and your profile details, may be shown to other users even when a setting says it is hidden, may be shown when you did not intend it, or may be shown inaccurately. Until Rider Wave's public release, treat anything you add to an account as information that other riders may be able to see. If that is not acceptable to you, do not create an account. The rest of the app works without an account. We fix privacy bugs as a priority when we find them.

How long we keep it, and deleting it. Account information is kept while your account exists. You can delete your account in the app at any time. Deleting it removes your profile, garage, phones, sign-ins, follows, blocks, posts, comments, likes and event replies, the routes you published, saved, rated or commented on, your completion marks, and the businesses where you are the only owner (with their pages, team and counts) from our server straight away; a business that has another owner stays with them. Deleting a single post removes it, with its likes, comments and replies, straight away. Reviewers can also remove a reported post or comment. Your name and notes are removed from the reports you made. Copies in our encrypted backups are deleted when those backups expire, within about two weeks. Your handle stays reserved for 30 days so no one else can take it at once. A report that a reviewer has decided is deleted 30 days after the decision. Data on your own phone stays until you delete it there.

Where it is kept. Account information is stored on the Rider Wave Server described in section 4, in the United States, and in its encrypted backups. Your personal information is not sold and not used for advertising. Combined ride-path data is described in section 8.

14. Law enforcement and legal requests

We will work with local, state and federal law enforcement agencies when we receive a valid warrant, court order or subpoena, or when the law otherwise requires us to give information. Outside of those cases, we will not give information about you to law enforcement or other government bodies. We can only give what we hold. Without an account that is very little (the short-lived access log in section 4); the rides, routes and places stored on your phone are not on our server.

15. Police officers can use Rider Wave too

Local, state and federal law enforcement officers can, and probably will, create their own Rider Wave accounts and profiles. Like any other rider they can see what is public, send friend (follow) requests, send messages where messaging is available, and add or remove police-presence icons on the map. Rider Wave does not control that, cannot tell who is an officer, and cannot stop it. Treat anything you share publicly, anything you accept from another account, and every police icon on the map as something a police officer may have added, changed or seen.

16. Changes to this policy

Rider Wave is under active development. Section 13 describes the kinds of information accounts and community features can use, and new features within those kinds are covered by it as they are added. We will update this policy before we begin collecting or sharing any kind of data not described here, and the "Last updated" date above shows when it last changed.

This document is a plain-language, accurate description of Rider Wave's actual current data practices as of the date above. It is a good-faith starting draft, not a substitute for review by a qualified attorney.
