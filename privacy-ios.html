Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026 · Applies to Rider Wave for iPhone

Plain-language summary: Rider Wave has no analytics and no advertising, and you can use it without an account. Without an account, your rides, routes and saved places stay on your iPhone. Some map features can be answered by the Rider Wave Server, which we run ourselves. When you use those, the server sees your IP address and the location or search text needed to answer, and keeps a basic access log, without those contents, for a short time, usually a few days. We never sell your data. Rider Wave accounts, profiles, business profiles and community features are optional and still being tested. If you create an account, the information you add, and anything you choose to share such as your location, heading, speed, route or destination, is stored on our server and may be seen by other riders. While these features are still being tested, the privacy settings that limit who sees it may not work correctly, so treat anything you add to an account as visible to other riders. Section 13 explains this in full. By using Rider Wave you accept this policy as part of the Terms of Use.

1. What this app is

Rider Wave is a motorcycle navigation and ride-tracking app under active development. Accounts are optional and still being tested (section 13); everything in sections 1 to 12 works without one. Without an account, your rides and settings are created and stored on your iPhone. For some map features the app can talk to the Rider Wave Server, described in section 4. Versions of Rider Wave published on Google Play or the App Store before November 2026 never contacted that server, and section 4 does not apply to them.

2. Data stored on your iPhone

The app stores the following in its own private storage on your iPhone:

  • Profile information you enter (name, rider icon, bike/garage entries)
  • Saved routes and ride history (GPS tracks, distance, time, speed)
  • Hazard reports and posts ("waves") you create
  • Saved places (home, work, favorites, recent searches and destinations)
  • Map pictures and weather data that the map keeps on your phone so it loads faster
  • Diagnostic log (off by default; Settings → Developer): if you turn it on, the app keeps a log of what it is doing, including your precise position and the time of each point, so problems can be investigated. It is held on your iPhone and leaves it only if you export it and choose where to send it
  • App settings

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS includes this data in those backups, as it does for most apps. You can delete this data at any time from Settings → Your data in the app, or by deleting the app.

3. Location data

With your permission, Rider Wave uses your iPhone's location to show your position on the map, give turn-by-turn directions, and record your ride while the app is open. It asks for location only while the app is in use; it does not track you in the background or while the screen is locked. Your ride track and position history are stored only on your iPhone. Location is sent off the iPhone only in the ways described in sections 4, 5 and 6, to answer a map request you make, and, if you have an account, when you choose to share it as described in section 13.

4. The Rider Wave Server

Rider Wave runs its own self-hosted server, operated by the app's developer on its own hardware in the United States, that can answer some map requests so that fewer of them go to outside companies. These features are listed below. All of them can be turned off in Settings → Developer; most are off by default, and "Search as you type" is on by default.

  • Routes (off by default): the start, stops and destination of a route that lies within Florida and Kentucky, and the direction you are heading when you are moving (so the route starts the right way), are sent so the server can calculate it. Other routes use the public routing services in section 6.
  • Address search (off by default): your search text and your position, used to rank nearby results.
  • Search as you type (on by default): the letters you have typed so far, and your position, to suggest places while you type. If you turn it off, searching works as before when you press Enter.
  • Weather radar (off by default): the radar map tiles for the area you are viewing.
  • Traffic rerouting suggestions (off by default): see section 5.

What the server records. Its access log keeps your IP address, the date and time, the type of request, the web address path, the response status, its size, how long it took and a few other technical details of the connection. For radar the path includes the map tile coordinates, so the log can show roughly which area was viewed. The log does not record the contents of requests (route coordinates, search text), your request headers, or query strings. Log entries are kept for a short time, usually a few days, and then deleted. The log is rotated by size, so on a very quiet server an entry could stay longer, but we aim to keep none for more than about a week. We use them only to keep the server secure, to limit abuse (for example, too many requests from one address) and to fix problems.

What the server does not do for these map features. They do not need an account, are not linked to one, do not identify you, and are not used to build profiles of riders. The app contains a shared access key that stops strangers from using the server; it is the same for every copy of the app and does not identify you. Connections use HTTPS.

5. Traffic rerouting suggestions and HERE

If you turn on "Traffic rerouting suggestions" (Settings → Developer), then while you are navigating Rider Wave sends your current position, the direction you are heading when you are moving, your destination, a few points along your route and roughly how far you have left to go to the Rider Wave Server every few minutes. The server asks HERE Technologies, a traffic data provider, for current traffic along that route and sends back only whether there is a slowdown and a possible faster route.

With the same switch on, the map also shows traffic on your route: when you look at a route before starting it, and every few minutes while you navigate, the app asks the Rider Wave Server for traffic in a few map squares of about 30 km that your route passes through (only the squares, not your position or destination). The server asks HERE for slow roads, closures, crashes and roadworks in those squares and passes them back, and the app draws them on the map. The server keeps these answers in memory for about two minutes and does not store them.

HERE receives those route coordinates, and the direction you are heading (so that facing the wrong way at the start of a route is not mistaken for a delay), from our server. When your phone does not send a direction, for example because you are stopped, the server works one out from the route's own points. It never receives your IP address, and the app has no account or device identifier to send it. The server keeps the answer in memory for about two minutes, so that nearby riders can share it, and does not store it. Rider Wave never changes your route unless you tap "Re-route". HERE's own privacy policy applies to what HERE receives.

6. Other services, sharing and links

When a feature is not using the Rider Wave Server, your iPhone sends the request directly to an independent service. Rider Wave does not operate these services, and each has its own privacy policy. They see your IP address, as any website does, and the information the request needs: for example the coordinates of a route and the direction you are heading, the area of the map you are viewing, or the place you searched for.

  • Nominatim / OpenStreetMap: address search and geocoding
  • Valhalla and OSRM: route calculation
  • Overpass API (OpenStreetMap): places nearby, such as gas stations
  • Open-Meteo: weather
  • U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov): weather alerts
  • RainViewer: radar imagery
  • Map tile providers, including OpenFreeMap, OpenStreetMap-based sources and Esri: the map imagery itself
  • CARTO: backup map imagery, used if the main map style cannot be loaded

Rider Wave uses no account or key of its own with these services.

Map pre-loading. To make the map feel instant, the app downloads map pictures ahead of time for the area around your current location: about 20 square miles for every map style when you open the map style picker (including Esri satellite imagery, even if you do not pick it), a wider area if you stay on a style, and the area around you for the weather view. Those requests go to the map providers listed above, and they show roughly where you are.

Sharing and links. When you tap Share (for example on a ride or a wave card), your phone's share sheet sends the text or link you chose to the app or person you choose; some of it may contain a place's coordinates, for example an OpenStreetMap link. Links such as "Official page", the website or this policy open in your browser or another app, which then sees what any website sees. Once you share something or follow a link, that app's or site's own privacy policy applies.

7. Music

If you allow access to Apple Music, Rider Wave shows the song playing in the Music app (title, artist, artwork, and position) on the map and sends play, pause, skip, and seek commands to it. It can also check which supported music apps are installed, so you can choose one to open from the map. For other music apps, iPhone only lets Rider Wave know that some audio is playing, never what it is. No music-service login is used, and none of this leaves your iPhone.

8. No analytics, no advertising, no sale of data

Rider Wave includes no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking software. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for advertising. We do not share what we receive except as described in sections 4 to 6 and 13, or if the law requires it.

9. How long we keep data, and your choices

  • On your iPhone: until you delete it in Settings → Your data or delete the app.
  • On the Rider Wave Server: access log entries are kept for a short time, usually a few days (we aim for no more than about a week), and then deleted. We do not intentionally store anything else about you.
  • You can turn off every server feature in Settings → Developer, deny or withdraw location permission in your system settings, or stop using the app.
  • If you have an account, you can delete it in the app at any time (section 13).
  • Map features are not linked to an account, so we generally cannot tell which log entries belong to you. If you would like to ask what we hold, or ask us to delete something, email us (section 12) and we will help where we can. If you live where privacy laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act apply: we do not sell or share personal information.

10. Security

We take reasonable steps to protect the server, including HTTPS, request limits and keeping the access log short-lived. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise it will never be compromised.

11. Children's privacy

Rider Wave is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Children should not create a Rider Wave account. If you believe a child has created one, contact us (section 12) and we will delete it.

12. Contact

Questions about this policy, or requests about data, can be sent to david@terrabae.com.

13. Rider Wave accounts, profiles and community features

Accounts are optional. Rider Wave works without one, and nothing in this section applies to you unless you create an account. Accounts are being tested and may not be available to you yet. When you create one, the app shows you the Terms of Use and this Privacy Policy and asks you to accept both before the account is made. Creating an account means you have read and accepted them, including the notice below about privacy settings.

What an account can hold. Depending on what you add and which features you use:

  • Your profile: handle (your public username), display name, bio, avatar colour or photo, cover photo, links, home region (city and state), the year you started riding, and your unit preference.
  • Your garage: bike nicknames, make, model, year, colour, photos, and, only if you choose to save it, the bike's VIN (stored encrypted and shown only to you).
  • Business profiles you create or join: the business name, handle, category, description, website, logo and photos, locations with address, map position, phone number and hours, the people who run it and their roles, and the documents you send to verify it.
  • Social activity: who you follow and who follows you, your posts, comments, likes, event replies, hazard reports, and the reports and blocks you make.
  • Location and riding information you choose to share: your current or live position, the direction you are heading, your speed, rides and routes you share (shown as a simplified line), your destination, places you stop, and ride totals such as distance and number of rides.
  • Account and security records: the phones signed in to your account (a name you choose, platform, app version, last-seen time and a public key created on your iPhone), sign-in sessions, a protected check value for your recovery code (never the words themselves), and the terms version you accepted.

We never ask for your email address, phone number, birthday, home address or contacts to create an account.

Who can see it. Your handle, display name and avatar are always visible to other riders. Everything else is meant to follow the privacy settings in the app (for example public, followers or private), and new settings start private. Business members, report reviewers and the people who run the Rider Wave Server can also see what their role requires.

Testing notice: privacy settings may not work. Accounts, profiles, business profiles, community features and their privacy settings are still being tested and have not been fully tested. The settings that control who can see your information are provided for testing only. Because of bugs or unfinished features, information you add to an account or share through it, including your location, the direction you are heading, your speed, your route, your destination, your home region, your bikes and your profile details, may be shown to other users even when a setting says it is hidden, may be shown when you did not intend it, or may be shown inaccurately. Until Rider Wave's public release, treat anything you add to an account as information that other riders may be able to see. If that is not acceptable to you, do not create an account, or do not turn on sharing features. The rest of the app works without an account. We fix privacy bugs as a priority when we find them.

How long we keep it, and deleting it. Account information is kept while your account exists. You can delete your account in the app at any time. Deleting it removes your profile, garage, devices and sessions from our server straight away; copies in our encrypted backups are deleted when those backups expire, within about two weeks; your handle stays reserved for 30 days so no one else can take it at once. Content that must remain for others, such as a business you co-own, is transferred or anonymised. Data on your own phone stays until you delete it there.

Where it is kept. Account information is stored on the Rider Wave Server described in section 4, in the United States, and in its encrypted backups. It is not sold and not used for advertising.

14. Changes to this policy

Rider Wave is under active development. Section 13 describes the kinds of information accounts and community features can use, and new features within those kinds are covered by it as they are added. We will update this policy before we begin collecting or sharing any kind of data not described here, and the "Last updated" date above shows when it last changed.
